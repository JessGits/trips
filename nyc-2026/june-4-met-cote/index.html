The Tone for Today

The first real day in the city. Take it slow this morning — pastry, the river, the appetizing shop everyone walks past. Then cross the park to the Met, the largest art museum in the Western Hemisphere, and spend a few hours inside. Hot dog on the steps after if anyone's hungry. Back uptown to freshen up. Cote at five — Korean BBQ done as fine dining, the kind of room that makes a memorable first dinner.

The Morning

8:30 AM Daily Provisions
375 Amsterdam Ave at W 78th · 2 blocks from The Wallace

Danny Meyer's neighborhood bakery. The maple cinnamon cruller is the move — it's the kind of pastry that ends arguments. Coffee is great, sandwiches don't matter yet, leave room for the Met steps. Sit at the counter or grab and walk to the river.

Twenty minutes of just walking. The path along the Hudson is one of the most underrated stretches in Manhattan — locals running, dogs being walked, the New Jersey skyline across the water. Walk south to about W 72nd, then cut east through the park back toward Broadway. The rest of the day is dense; this is the quiet stretch.

10:00 AM Zabar's
2245 Broadway at W 80th · open since 1934

An institution. New York's most legendary "appetizing" shop — smoked fish, bagels, babka, cheeses, coffee, kitchen gadgets upstairs. Walk through, look at the lox counter, see what calls to you. Coffee and a bite at the cafe is fair game if anyone wants — just remember the food tour is tomorrow and Cote is tonight. A babka or a Zabar's tote makes a good souvenir.

🍴 Bourdain · A neighborhood note

Anthony Bourdain's actual neighborhood breakfast spot — Barney Greengrass, the Sturgeon King — is six blocks north on Amsterdam at 86th. He called it "the best breakfast in the universe" and ate there nearly every Sunday he was home. We're saving it for another morning. Just know it's there.

The Met

🚌
Cross the park to the Met — M79 crosstown bus from Broadway & 79th to 5th Ave & 79th · 15 min · or a 25-min walk across Central Park if the weather is gorgeous
10:30 AM The Metropolitan Museum of Art
1000 5th Avenue at E 82nd · timed entry, 4 General Admission · ~$30/adult

The biggest museum in the Western Hemisphere has two million objects. You can't see them. Pick a few rooms, let the rest go. Plan on roughly two and a half to three hours, but if anyone wants to keep going, the Met will keep going with you — just remember Cote at five.

One good arc:

Egyptian Wing. The Temple of Dendur is the headliner — a real Egyptian temple reassembled inside the museum, with a reflecting pool and a wall of windows facing Central Park. Walk through it.

Arms & Armor. Mounted knights, samurai armor, parade armor for Renaissance princes. One of the best rooms in any museum in the world.

American Wing. Washington Crossing the Delaware is the painting that's bigger than you think. The American period rooms — entire 1700s living rooms transplanted into the museum — are also here.

The rooftop. The Cantor Roof Garden if it's open in early June — views over Central Park, a small bar. Worth checking on the way out.

A natural split for Raphael

Raphael: Sublime Poetry — the first-ever U.S. retrospective of the Renaissance master — closes June 28. It's the once-in-a-lifetime kind of exhibition, and it's a deep-cut. If it calls to you, peel off for ~45 minutes; everyone else can drift toward Arms & Armor. Reconvene at the American Wing around 12:15. Phones on, keep loose contact. The Met is enormous but the wings are well-signed.

After the Met Hot Dog on the Steps
The Met steps · 5th Ave · between 80th and 84th

If anyone's hungry coming out, the hot dog carts at the bottom of the steps are a small ritual — the steps are wide enough to seat half a neighborhood and people have been eating dogs on them for generations. Eight bucks. Twenty minutes. Watch the city.

If everyone's full from breakfast, skip it. Cote at five is a serious meal.

The Drift Back

2:00 — 3:30 PM Central Park & Madison Drift
Loose path · Central Park east side · Madison Avenue south

Cross into Central Park behind the Met. Walk thirty minutes — the Great Lawn, the Reservoir if anyone's up for it, the east-side paths. No agenda. When energy starts to flag, exit at 79th or 72nd onto Madison Ave and drift south, peering into shop windows. Madison between 65th and 80th is where the city goes to spend money quietly. You don't have to buy anything.

Subway home: grab the 6 train from any Lexington Ave stop in the 70s and transfer at 59th-Columbus Circle to the 1, or just rideshare back if anyone's wiped. ~$15 cab to The Wallace.

The Evening

🚇
1 train from 72nd to 23rd St · 6 stops · about 15 min · or rideshare ~$20 if running late
5:00 PM Cote
16 W 22nd St · Flatiron · Michelin · party of 4 · Resy ✅

One of the most fun rooms in New York. Cote is a Korean steakhouse that won a Michelin star for treating Korean BBQ as fine dining — the grill is set into the table, the cuts of meat are prime dry-aged American beef, and the service is theatrical without being precious. The dining room is dim, low ceilings, leather booths, glowing grills throwing flickering light. Loud-ish but in a good way.

Order the Butcher's Feast ($72/person) — four cuts of beef cooked at your table, a stew, egg soufflé, sides, and soft-serve to finish. It's the move. Zero decisions. No foam, no tweezers, just exceptional food at the right pace. The 5pm slot is the early seating — quieter than peak, the room slowly fills around you as you eat.
