The Tone for Today

The first real day in the city. Pastry to start, then a slow walk through Central Park — Strawberry Fields, Bethesda Fountain, Bow Bridge — ending at the steps of the Met. A few hours inside. Hot dog on the steps if anyone's hungry. Back uptown to freshen up. Cote at five.

The Morning

8:30 AM Daily Provisions
375 Amsterdam Ave at W 78th · 2 blocks from The Wallace

Danny Meyer's neighborhood bakery. The maple cinnamon cruller is the move — it's the kind of pastry that ends arguments. Coffee is great, sandwiches don't matter yet, leave room for the Met steps.

~9:15 AM Strawberry Fields
72nd St entrance · west side of Central Park · the John Lennon memorial

The first thing inside the park. The black-and-white Imagine mosaic set into the path, surrounded by quiet trees. Lennon lived across the street at the Dakota and was shot in front of it in 1980 — the city dedicated this stretch of the park to him a few years later. There's almost always a musician here playing Beatles songs. A two-minute pause, sometimes longer if the music's good.

🎬 On screen

The Dakota — directly across Central Park West from the memorial — is the apartment building from Rosemary's Baby (1968), and the place John Lennon was returning home to when he was shot in 1980. Anyone reading about the building's haunted reputation knows this view.

~9:30 AM The Mall & Literary Walk
central spine of the park · cathedral canopy of American elms

The only straight path in Central Park. A quarter-mile-long promenade lined with American elm trees that arch overhead into a cathedral canopy — one of the largest remaining stands of elms in North America. Statues of writers (Shakespeare, Burns, Scott, Halleck) line the way. It opens onto Bethesda Terrace at the end.

~9:50 AM Bethesda Terrace & Fountain
the heart of Central Park · arriving from the Mall

One of the most filmed outdoor locations in New York. The Angel of the Waters bronze fountain at the center, set in front of a grand terrace with arched colonnades. Often there's a busker singing under the arches with the acoustics doing the work.

Look up — the terrace ceiling is 15,000 hand-painted Minton tiles, the only tile ceiling of its kind in the world. Most people walk under it without ever noticing.

~10:05 AM Bow Bridge
a short walk west from Bethesda · cast-iron bridge across the Lake

An elegant cast-iron bridge arching over the Lake, with the Ramble rising on the far side. From Bethesda, walk along the lakeshore to your west and it's right there.

The Met

10:30 AM The Metropolitan Museum of Art
1000 5th Avenue at E 82nd · timed entry, 4 General Admission · ~$30/adult

The biggest museum in the Western Hemisphere has two million objects. You can't see them. Pick a few rooms, let the rest go. Plan on roughly two and a half to three hours, but if anyone wants to keep going, the Met will keep going with you — just remember Cote at five.

One good arc: Egyptian Wing → Arms & Armor → American Wing → the rooftop (Cantor Roof Garden) on the way out if it's open.

📍Open in Maps
A natural split for Raphael

Raphael: Sublime Poetry — the first-ever U.S. retrospective of the Renaissance master — closes June 28. It's the once-in-a-lifetime kind of exhibition, and it's a deep-cut. If it calls to you, peel off for ~45 minutes; everyone else can drift toward Arms & Armor. Reconvene at the American Wing around 12:15. Phones on, keep loose contact. The Met is enormous but the wings are well-signed.

🎬 On screen

Ocean's Eight (2018) — the Met Gala heist; Debbie Ocean's crew lifts a Cartier necklace from Daphne Kluger in the bathroom. The great hall, the Egyptian Wing, and the front steps all feature.

Gossip Girl — the Met steps are the show's most-filmed exterior; Blair Waldorf holds court here daily.

When Harry Met Sally (1989) — Harry and Sally walk the Temple of Dendur, mid-friendship, talking about their lives.

Also I Am Legend (the steps, deserted) and many more — the Met has been New York shorthand on film since the 1950s.

After the Met Hot Dog on the Steps
The Met steps · 5th Ave · between 80th and 84th

If anyone's hungry coming out, the hot dog carts at the bottom of the steps are a small ritual. About $8. Twenty minutes on the steps watching the city.

Skip without worry if everyone's still full from breakfast.

The Drift Back

Cross into Central Park behind the Met · loose path south on the east side

Cross into Central Park directly behind the Met and walk south along the east-side paths. Thirty minutes, no agenda — the Great Lawn, the Reservoir if anyone's curious, benches if anyone needs to sit.

~2:45 PM Central Park Zoo · if it calls
5th Ave at 64th St · ~$20/adult at the gate · small, walkable

Small by zoo standards — a sea lion pool you can walk around in five minutes, a snow leopard exhibit, the Tropic Zone rainforest, the penguin house. 30–45 minutes is plenty. Skip if it doesn't call.

5:00 PM Cote
16 W 22nd St · Flatiron · Michelin · party of 4 · Resy ✅

One of the most fun rooms in New York. Cote is a Korean steakhouse that won a Michelin star for treating Korean BBQ as fine dining — the grill is set into the table, the cuts of meat are prime dry-aged American beef, and the service is theatrical without being precious. The dining room is dim, low ceilings, leather booths, glowing grills throwing flickering light.

The Butcher's Feast is the fan favorite if it helps the table decide — four cuts of beef cooked at your table, a stew, egg soufflé, sides. The 5pm slot is the early seating, with the room filling around you as you eat.

One thing to know: the meal ends with soft-serve. It's the surprise nobody talks about, and people who pace badly miss it. Save a little room.
