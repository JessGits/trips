10:00 AM — 12:30 PM Circle Line · Best of New York
Pier 83 · W 43rd St & 12th Ave · 2.5 hours · fully around Manhattan

The classic harbor cruise — first runs in 1945, still one of the best ways to see the city. The full loop goes south along the Hudson, around the Statue of Liberty for the closest legal view, past the World Trade Center and the financial district, up the East River under all the bridges (Brooklyn, Manhattan, Williamsburg), and back down via the Harlem River and the Hudson.

Live narration on board — usually a New Yorker who knows the city inside out. Bar and snack counter downstairs.

📍Open in Maps
✨ Hidden gem

Sit on the right side (starboard) heading out. The Statue of Liberty appears on the right as the boat rounds the Battery, and the views of lower Manhattan and the financial district are best from that side. Coming back up the Hudson, swap to the left for the West Side / midtown skyline.

✨ Hidden gem

The boat passes very close to "The Little Red Lighthouse" under the George Washington Bridge — subject of the famous 1942 children's book. Look for the small red lighthouse dwarfed by the massive grey bridge above. Most riders miss it.

The Afternoon · Lunch & Intrepid

~12:45 PM Lunch near the Piers
Hell's Kitchen · walking distance from Pier 83

Walk east on 43rd or 46th into Hell's Kitchen — 9th and 10th Avenues between 42nd and 54th have one of the densest restaurant strips in Manhattan. A few solid options:

· Empanada Mama (763 9th Ave) — family-run, dozens of empanadas, quick and casual.
· Totto Ramen (366 W 52nd) — cash-only, no reservations, one of the most-loved ramen shops in NYC. Usually a short line at lunch.
· Los Tacos No. 1 — there's a location at the Intrepid Museum itself if you want to combine lunch and the next stop. Authentic LA-style tacos, cash and card.
· Sullivan Street Bakery (533 W 47th) — pizza al taglio (by the slice), Roman style, perfect for grab-and-go.

Keep it light — the Intrepid is on your feet.

🚶
Walk to the Intrepid. Pier 86 is 3 blocks north of Pier 83 (W 46th & 12th Ave). ~5 min walk along the river.
~2:00 PM Intrepid Sea, Air & Space Museum
Pier 86 · W 46th St at the Hudson · closes 5 PM

An actual aircraft carrier turned museum — the USS Intrepid served in WWII, the Vietnam War, and as a recovery vessel for Mercury and Gemini space missions. Now permanently docked at Pier 86 with three other vessels and an indoor exhibit hall.

The three main attractions:

· The flight deck — 28 aircraft including the Lockheed A-12 Blackbird (the predecessor to the SR-71, top speed Mach 3.3) and the F-14 Tomcat made famous by Top Gun.
· The Space Shuttle Pavilion — home to the Space Shuttle Enterprise, the prototype that paved the way for all the operational shuttles. NASA's first.
· USS Growler — a Cold War-era submarine docked alongside. Climb in. The interior is tight — if anyone's claustrophobic, the flight deck and Space Shuttle pavilion are the priorities.

Plan ~2.5 hours minimum. Tickets should be booked in advance to skip the line.

📍Open in Maps
✨ Hidden gem

The Concorde sits outside on the pier — British Airways' supersonic passenger jet, retired in 2003. New York-Paris in 3.5 hours. You can walk around it but can't go inside without a separate timed ticket (worth it for aviation enthusiasts).

🎬 On screen

The Intrepid has appeared in Transformers: Dark of the Moon (2011), The Avengers (2012) background shots, and National Treasure 2 (2007).

The neighborhood (Hell's Kitchen / Pier 83-86 area) is also where the climactic Hudson River sequence of Sully (2016) was filmed — the actual Miracle on the Hudson landing happened a few hundred yards south of here in January 2009.
