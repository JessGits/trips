~10:00 AM Brooklyn Bridge
1.1 mile walk · ~30–40 min slow pace · pedestrian walkway above the traffic

Opened 1883 — the first steel-wire suspension bridge in the world. The wooden pedestrian walkway runs above the cars and offers some of the best views of both the Manhattan and Brooklyn skylines, plus the Statue of Liberty in the distance.

Walk on the south side (right side heading toward Brooklyn) for the best Manhattan skyline views as you turn around. Bikes have their own lane — stay clear.

🎬 On screen

Annie Hall (1977) — the iconic morning-after-stroll across the bridge.

Once Upon a Time in America (1984), Saturday Night Fever (1977), Sex and the City (TV) — the bridge has been in nearly every NYC-set film over the past 50 years.

I Am Legend (2007) — Will Smith on the abandoned, post-apocalyptic bridge.

✨ Hidden gem

Stop at the first stone tower from the Manhattan side — the plaques carved into the granite include the bridge's history and the names of the Roebling family who designed and built it. Emily Roebling effectively completed the bridge after her husband fell ill, becoming the first person to cross it.

🚶
Exit the bridge and descend into DUMBO. Take the stairs or walk down via Washington St. ~5 min walk to Old Fulton St area.
~10:45 AM DUMBO & Brooklyn Bridge Park
Down Under the Manhattan Bridge Overpass · cobblestone streets, waterfront, photo ops

An old industrial waterfront turned art-and-design neighborhood. Cobblestone streets, converted warehouses (the Empire Stores are now a food hall and shops), the East River on one side and the Manhattan and Brooklyn Bridges framing the sky.

✨ Hidden gem

Washington Street between Front and Water Streets — the photo. Stand here and the Manhattan Bridge frames perfectly between the brick buildings, with the Empire State in the distance. The most-photographed spot in Brooklyn for a reason. Expect a small line of tourists waiting their turn.

✨ Hidden gem

Jane's Carousel — a fully restored 1922 carousel inside a glass pavilion right on the waterfront. $3 a ride, open today (closed Tuesdays). Whether you ride or just admire from outside, it's a quiet moment between the bridges.

11:30 AM Juliana's
19 Old Fulton St · coal-fired oven · cash and card · opens at 11:30 sharp

Patsy Grimaldi opened the original Grimaldi's here, sold it, then came back at 81 and opened Juliana's two doors down (named after his mother). Most New Yorkers will tell you Juliana's is the better of the two. Coal-fired thin-crust, blistered bottom, fresh mozzarella.

Arrive at opening (11:30 AM) — the line builds fast. They don't take reservations.

Fan favorites: the classic Margherita, the No. 1 (white pie with arugula and prosciutto added after), and the No. 6 (with grilled chicken and homemade guacamole — surprisingly great). Order whole pies, not slices.

~2:00 PM VITAL Climbing Williamsburg
221 N 14th St, Brooklyn · bouldering-only · rooftop climbing

Anthony's climb. Day pass + rental shoes. The waiver must be filled out in advance online — check before leaving the Wallace.

VITAL has wall-to-wall bouldering on the main floor and outdoor rooftop climbing with skyline views. There's a communal table by the entrance with seating, and the climbing floor has spots to sit and watch. Plan ~2 hours.

The plan: hang out, watch Anthony climb, take it slow. If anyone wants to pop out for a bit during the session, North Williamsburg is right outside — see below.

If dinner near the Cellar Minetta Tavern
113 MacDougal St · literally next door to the Cellar (2 doors south)

Open since 1937 — the original red-checkered tablecloth Village bar, reborn by Keith McNally in 2009. Black-and-white tile floor, red leather banquettes, framed boxing photos on the wall. The Black Label Burger (dry-aged blend, caramelized onions, served on a brioche bun) is one of the most famous burgers in NYC.

Reservations strongly recommended — if not, the bar is walk-in-friendly and serves the full menu.

📍Open in Maps
🍴 Bourdain

Bourdain loved Minetta — called the Black Label Burger one of his favorite burgers in the city. He shot here for The Layover and brought visiting chefs through when they were in town.

9:30 PM Comedy Cellar · MacDougal Room
117 MacDougal St · the original room · underground, low ceilings, tight

Opened 1982. The most legendary comedy club in NYC — Seinfeld, Chappelle, Burr, Patton Oswalt, all the SNL writers, drop in unannounced regularly. The lineup is kept secret until you're inside. Expect 5–7 comics over ~90 minutes.

How it works: Show up 15–20 min early. Photo IDs at the door — mandatory. Two-item minimum per person (drinks or food, your call). Phones get sealed in Yondr pouches on the way in. The room is small — you'll be close to the comics.

📍Open in Maps
✨ Hidden gem

Caffe Reggio is next door at 119 MacDougal. Open until 3 AM — if anyone wants to peel off and skip the Cellar (the room is genuinely tight, basement-style), the world's first American espresso machine is two doors away. Open since 1927, featured in The Godfather Part II. Coffee, dessert, history. Easy meeting point for after the show.
