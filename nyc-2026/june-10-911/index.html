9:00 AM 9/11 Memorial & Museum
180 Greenwich St · entry at 9 AM · 4 tickets · self-guided

The museum sits seven stories underground, on the original foundations of the Twin Towers. The exhibits trace the morning of September 11, 2001, and the days that followed, through artifacts, recordings, photographs, and personal stories.

It is a self-guided visit — move at your own pace, take the time you need, step out when you want to. Plan two to three hours. There is no wrong amount of time to spend.

📍Open in Maps

The museum is emotionally heavy by design. Some galleries — particularly the historical exhibition — hold difficult images and recordings. It's completely fine to pass through any section quickly, or to wait in the larger memorial spaces while others continue. There are quiet benches throughout.

After the museum The Memorial Pools
Outdoors at street level · the footprints of the North and South Towers

Two reflecting pools sit in the exact footprints of the fallen towers, each nearly an acre, with water cascading down all four sides into a center void. The names of every person killed in the 2001 and 1993 attacks are inscribed in bronze around the edges.

The pools are free and open to the public — a calm, open space after the museum below.

✨ Worth knowing

Look for the white roses placed in carved names — the Memorial staff place a rose at each person's name on their birthday. If you see one, it's someone's birthday today. A small, ongoing act of remembrance.

Optional, 2 blocks east St. Paul's Chapel
209 Broadway · across from the WTC site · open till 6 PM, free

The oldest church building in Manhattan in continuous use (1766). It stood directly across from the Twin Towers and survived September 11 without a single broken window. In the months after, it became a round-the-clock relief center for recovery workers — volunteers served meals, and cots filled the pews.

Inside, a quiet exhibit preserves that chapter. Worth the two-block walk if anyone wants it.

The Afternoon · Financial District

~12:30 PM The Oculus
World Trade Center Transportation Hub · Santiago Calatrava, 2016

A transit hub and shopping center that looks like nothing else in the city — a vast white ribbed structure meant to evoke a dove released from a child's hands. The main hall (the Oculus itself) is a soaring white cathedral of a space, all marble and light.

There's food in the lower level if anyone wants a quick lunch — Eataly is upstairs, and the food court has plenty of fast options.

📍Open in Maps
✨ Worth knowing

Every September 11, the Oculus skylight is opened to the sky — the structure is aligned so that on that morning, a beam of light falls along the main hall between 8:46 and 10:28 AM, the span of time between the first plane and the second tower's fall.

🚶
A loop through the Financial District. The next few stops are walking distance — the whole area is compact, cobblestone in parts, dense with history. ~15 min of total walking, more if you linger.
~1:30 PM Wall Street & Federal Hall
26 Wall St · the NYSE is across the street · free, open till 4 PM

The New York Stock Exchange sits at the corner of Wall and Broad — you can't go in, but the famous columned facade with the giant flag is right there. Across the street, Federal Hall is where George Washington took the oath as the first president in 1789, and where the Bill of Rights was passed. The bronze statue of Washington on the steps marks the spot.

📍Open in Maps
~2:00 PM Charging Bull
Bowling Green · south end of Broadway

The 7,000-pound bronze bull was installed without permission in 1989 — the artist, Arturo Di Modica, dropped it in front of the Stock Exchange overnight as guerrilla art after the 1987 crash, a symbol of resilience. The city tried to remove it; public outcry kept it. It now lives a couple blocks south at Bowling Green.

Expect a line for the photo — both ends of the bull are popular.

If anyone wants it Ghostbusters Firehouse
14 N Moore St, Tribeca · ~12 min north of the WTC site

For anyone who wants it, the Hook & Ladder 8 firehouse — the Ghostbusters headquarters — is a short walk north into Tribeca. It's a real, working FDNY station, immediately recognizable. A quick sidewalk photo; don't block the bay doors.

It's slightly off the direct path home, so it fits best as a detour for the film fans rather than a group stop.

Dinner Charles Pan-Fried Chicken
146 W 72nd St · 2 blocks from the Wallace · walk-in

Charles Gabriel pan-fries his chicken to order in a cast-iron skillet — the old way, one batch at a time, the way he learned growing up in North Carolina. After decades of running the operation out of Harlem (and a food truck), this is his sit-down spot. The chicken is the order; the sides — mac and cheese, collard greens, candied yams, cornbread — are the supporting cast.

Casual, counter-service-leaning, walk-in. The right kind of comfort food to land on after a heavy day.

📍Open in Maps
✨ Worth knowing

Charles Gabriel won a James Beard Award and his pan-fried chicken has been called the best in the city by critics who've been chasing it for years. The catfish is a Sunday/Tuesday special — if it's on, regulars say it rivals the chicken.
