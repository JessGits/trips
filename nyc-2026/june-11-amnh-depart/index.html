10:00 AM American Museum of Natural History
200 Central Park West at 79th · timed entry · 4 tickets

45 exhibition halls across 5 floors — one of the largest natural history museums in the world. You can't do all of it; pick a few floors and let them be the ones.

The four anchors:

· Hall of Ocean Life (1st floor) — the 94-foot blue whale suspended from the ceiling, surrounded by ocean dioramas. The whole room is quietly extraordinary.
· Hall of African Mammals (1st floor) — the centerpiece elephant herd diorama. Still one of the great museum experiences in the city.
· Fossil Halls / Hall of Dinosaurs (4th floor) — T-rex and Apatosaurus reconstructions, genuine fossils, the Barosaurus rearing in the Roosevelt Rotunda.
· Hall of Planet Earth (1st floor, Rose Center) — on the way to the Planetarium. Meteorites, specimens, the rock of the Earth's crust.

🎬 On screen

Night at the Museum (2006), Night at the Museum: Battle of the Smithsonian (2009), Night at the Museum: Secret of the Tomb (2014) — the entire trilogy is set here. Ben Stiller's security guard watches the exhibits come to life. The Roosevelt Rotunda, Hall of African Mammals, and Fossil Hall are all recognizable.

The Squid and the Whale (2005) — Noah Baumbach set key scenes in the museum.

✨ Hidden gem

The meteorite collection in the Hall of Planet Earth has a 34-ton iron meteorite you can touch — the Ahnighito, one of the largest meteorites ever found, pulled from the Arctic by Peary in 1897. Most people walk past it. It's cold to the touch.

~12:00 PM Hayden Planetarium · Encounters in the Milky Way
Rose Center for Earth and Space · separate ticket · ~24 min show

The Space Theater sits inside the Hayden Sphere — a 429-seat dome with a full-ceiling digital projection system. Encounters in the Milky Way narrated by Lupita Nyong'o, takes you from the Solar System out through the galaxy. About 24 minutes. Check the show schedule and book in advance.

📍Open in Maps
✨ Hidden gem

The glass wall facing 81st Street shows the Scales of the Universe exhibit — a spiral ramp descending around the outside of the Sphere, with scale models of cosmic objects from the size of the universe down to a single proton. Walk it on the way in or out. Undervisited and genuinely extraordinary.
