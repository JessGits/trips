10:15 AM — ARRIVE Katz's Delicatessen · Nice Guy Tours meeting point
205 E Houston at Ludlow · 4 tickets ✅ · cheese allergy noted

Arrive 15 minutes early — by 10:15 AM. Katz's has coffee, restrooms, and seating; feel free to wait inside. The guide will check inside and right out front of Katz's for the group. Tour kicks off at 10:30. Come hungry.

The oldest, loudest, most iconic deli in New York. Opened in 1888 and they have not budged. Pastrami hand-carved at the counter in front of you. Salami hanging from the ceiling, neon signs that look unchanged since the 60s, a ticket system you'll figure out instantly. The tour covers the pastrami sandwich and likely a half-sour pickle on the side.

🍴 Bourdain

Katz's was on Bourdain's permanent short list. He filmed here for Disappearing Manhattan and put it in his Tech Insider top-three NYC delis. His order: pastrami on rye with sour pickles and a Dr. Brown's Cream Soda.

His take on Katz's longevity, paraphrased: when you're the best and everyone agrees you're the best, you tend to stay open.

🎬 On screen

When Harry Met Sally · "I'll have what she's having" — the table where Meg Ryan delivered the most famous deli scene in movie history is marked with a small sign hanging from the ceiling. Look up.

~11:15 AM Russ & Daughters
179 E Houston at Allen · since 1914

Across Houston from Katz's. The original "appetizing" shop — an entire category of Jewish food store that's basically gone everywhere else. Founded in 1914 by Joel Russ; named for his three daughters when he made them partners, which was radical at the time. Smoked salmon hand-sliced wafer-thin behind a marble counter, herring in cream sauce, sturgeon, sable, whitefish salad, bagels, bialys. The tour usually does a tasting of cured fish.

📍Open in Maps
🍴 Bourdain

The other Bourdain top-three deli. He came here on Parts Unknown with author Joel Rose to eat what he called the "timeless New York classic." His order: a bagel with cream cheese and lox. His on-camera reaction: "Is there anything more New York?"

The shop opens directly into the cure room, and the wafer-thin slicing technique is the same one Joel Russ used in the 1920s.

~11:45 AM Kossar's Bagels & Bialys
367 Grand St · since 1936

The last surviving original bialy bakery in America. A bialy is a flatter cousin of the bagel — chewier, with a depression in the middle filled with caramelized onion instead of a hole. Kossar's hand-rolls them, boards them, and bakes them on a hearth oven the same way they have for ninety years. The bialy is the move. Bagels are great too; the bialy is the rarer thing.

~12:15 PM The Pickle Guys
357 Grand St · old-world brine

The last working pickle shop in the historic LES pickle district, which used to have dozens. Barrels of pickles in saltwater brine on the sidewalk, cured the old way — no vinegar, no refrigeration, just salt and time. You'll taste the spectrum: new pickles (fresh, garlicky), half-sours (a few weeks in), full-sours (months). Also pickled tomatoes, peppers, watermelon rind, mango. They'll let you try anything.

📍Open in Maps
~12:45 PM Doughnut Plant
379 Grand St · since 1994

Founded in a basement on the LES in 1994 by Mark Israel using his grandfather's recipe. Now a small empire, but the original is still here and still the best one. The square jelly doughnut is the signature — jam piped into all four corners so every bite gets some. The tres leches and crème brûlée doughnuts are also serious. Sweet finish to the tour.

The Drift North

1:30 — 4:30 PM SoHo · Nolita · Tribeca
Walk west and north · no agenda · pick where to wander

The neighborhoods just north and west of where the tour ends are some of the most walkable in New York. No plan, no list. Drift through SoHo's cast-iron architecture and big-brand boutiques. Cross into Nolita for narrow streets and smaller shops, cafés on corners, a different pace. Then Tribeca: cobblestones, converted warehouses, quieter than SoHo. Anyone tired can hop in a cab whenever; anyone wanting to keep going can chase the next interesting street.

If you wander west through Tribeca — the Ghostbusters firehouse at 14 N Moore St is on the way. A real working FDNY station (Hook & Ladder 8), red brick, immediately recognizable. Quick sidewalk photo, don't block the bay doors, five minutes total. Skip it if your drift goes elsewhere.

Hook & Ladder 8 · 14 N Moore St — the exterior firehouse in Ghostbusters (1984), Ghostbusters II (1989), Ghostbusters (2016). Also appears in I Am Legend. Interior scenes were Hollywood sets.

7:00 PM Dog Day Afternoon
August Wilson Theatre · 245 W 52nd St · 4 tickets ✅

The Broadway adaptation of the 1975 Sidney Lumet film — the true story of a Brooklyn bank robbery gone wrong, originally played by Al Pacino. This production stars Jon Bernthal (The Punisher, The Bear) as Sonny and Ebon Moss-Bachrach (Cousin Richie in The Bear) as his accomplice Sal. Two of the best dramatic actors of their generation, in close quarters, in a story that's tense from the first minute. Roughly 2.5 hours including intermission.

The August Wilson is mid-sized — capacity around 1,200 — so even modest seats feel close to the stage. Phones away the moment the lights drop. Out by 9:30.
