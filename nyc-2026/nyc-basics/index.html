First Time in NYC

The Locals' Briefing

Read this on the plane. Everything you need to move through the city the way New Yorkers do — no tourist fumbles, no surprises.

The Subway

📱
OMNY tap-to-pay Tap your iPhone or Apple Watch at the turnstile. No MetroCard, no app, no setup. It just works. $2.90 a ride. The system charges fairly if you tap in and out consistently. Android works too. Google Pay, Samsung Pay, any contactless card.
🗺️
Uptown = north, Downtown = south That's how New Yorkers give directions. "Take the 1 downtown to 72nd" means take the 1 train heading south and get off at 72nd Street. If you're not sure which direction to board, check the platform sign for the end-of-line destination.
1️⃣
The 1 train is your line The 1 stops at 72nd & Broadway (5-min walk from The Wallace) and runs straight downtown with no transfers. It gets you everywhere you need to go on this trip. The 2 and 3 run on the same track but skip some local stops. At 72nd, all three stop.
🚇
The subway runs 24 hours It does not shut down. Service is less frequent late at night but it always runs. After the Comedy Cellar, you can take the 1 home from Christopher St at 1 AM and it will come.
📶
Download Apple Maps offline before you go On iOS: open Apple Maps → search NYC → tap the download button on the city overview. You'll have full maps without signal. The subway has no cell service underground.

Getting Around Above Ground

🚕
Yellow cabs are often faster than Uber in Manhattan Uber has to navigate to you first. A yellow cab is already there. Hail one on any major avenue — if the rooftop number is lit, it's available. Pay by card or cash; card readers are in every cab. Uber still wins for LGA airport runs and any trip to Brooklyn — there you want a confirmed price.
🧱
Block math: short blocks vs. long blocks Streets run east-west and are short — about a minute to walk a block. Avenues run north-south and are long — about 3-4 minutes per block. "Three blocks" means something very different depending on which direction you're walking. A good rule: 10 north-south blocks = about 1 mile = about 20 minutes walking.
🚶
Walk on the right, pass on the left Same rule as driving. Stopping in the middle of the sidewalk to look at your phone is the one thing that genuinely annoys New Yorkers. Step to the side.

Tipping

🍽️
Restaurants: 20% is standard, 18% is the floor The easiest math: double the tax line on your receipt (NYC tax is 8.875%) and that's close to 18%. Add a bit more for 20%. Tip on the pre-tax total, not the full bill. The card machine will suggest inflated amounts — you can enter a custom tip.
☕
Coffee / counter service: $1–2 per order The tip screen will show 18-20-25% options. For a $5 coffee, $1 is fine. You don't have to tip at all for counter service but most people leave something.
🚕
Cabs and rideshare: 15–20% The app or screen will calculate it. 15% is acceptable, 20% for good service or heavy traffic.
🛎️
Hotel housekeeping: $3–5/day Leave it on the pillow or nightstand each morning. Cash only. Not required but appreciated.

Restrooms

Public restrooms in NYC are genuinely hard to find. There are almost none in subway stations. The ones that exist are often closed or not worth using. The system locals use:

☕
Starbucks Scan the app to unlock the bathroom door. Works in every Starbucks. There's one on nearly every other block.
🏨
Hotel lobbies Walk in confidently, find the lobby restroom. Works every time. Midtown hotels are the most reliable — The Plaza, Marriott, Hilton lobbies are all fair game.
🏛️
Museum cafés Any museum we're visiting has good restrooms inside. Use them on the way in and out.
🛍️
The Oculus / Brookfield Place The WTC transportation hub has clean, easy-to-find restrooms on the lower level.

The move on a full day: hit a Starbucks or museum before a long stretch of walking. You'll thank yourself on the Brooklyn Bridge.

Cash & Cards

💳
NYC is almost entirely card-friendly You can go days without touching cash. Tap-to-pay works on the subway, in cabs, at most restaurants and shops.
💵
Cash-only spots to know A few places on this trip specifically need cash: Joe's Pizza (June 6), Katz's Deli tip (the tour covers the food, but tip in cash), and a few market vendors. $60 in small bills covers it comfortably.
🏧
ATMs are everywhere, fees are annoying Use your bank's app to find in-network ATMs. Chase, Citi, and TD ATMs are on almost every block in Manhattan. The bodegas have ATMs but the fees are high.

Scams & Skips

Most of these are around Times Square, which isn't on our itinerary. But you'll pass through the area a few times.

💿
The CD guys Someone hands you a "free" CD or mixtape, then demands money. Don't take it. Decline politely and keep walking.
🎭
Free comedy show flyers Someone on the street near Times Square offers you a "free" ticket to a comedy show. The show exists. The drink minimum is not free. Skip it — we have Comedy Cellar.
📸
Costumed characters in Times Square Elmo, Spider-Man, Minnie Mouse — they will take a photo with you and then demand a tip. It's not a scam exactly, just aggressive. If you take the photo, tip a few dollars. If you don't want the photo, don't make eye contact.
🏀
The shell game and three-card monte Street gambling, usually with a small crowd. The crowd is in on it. Don't play.

June Weather

🌡️
Highs in the low-to-mid 70s, humid Comfortable for walking but the humidity is real. Morning is the best time to be outside; afternoons get sticky.
⛈️
Afternoon thunderstorms are possible June in NYC gets pop-up storms that last 30–45 minutes and then clear. Keep a compact umbrella or light rain jacket in a bag. Don't cancel anything — they pass fast.
❄️
AC is aggressive indoors Restaurants, museums, and shops are often cold enough to need a layer. The Met, the Intrepid, and the Cellar all run heavy AC. One light layer in a bag covers it.
💨
Top of the Rock is windy Even on a warm evening, the open-air observation deck at Rockefeller Center gets a real wind. Bring something to put on at the top.

How New York Works

🕐
Everything is open later than you think Bars serve until 4 AM. Restaurants take reservations until 10 PM. Delis and bodegas never close. The subway runs all night.
🗣️
New Yorkers are direct, not rude If you ask someone for directions, you'll get a fast, accurate, no-nonsense answer. That's not coldness — that's how people who live in a dense city with a lot to do communicate. Ask away.
🐾
New York has a lot of dogs The Upper West Side especially. Big dogs, small dogs, dogs in strollers. Normal.
🥯
A New York bagel is a different thing The water, the boiling process, the texture — it genuinely doesn't taste the same anywhere else. Have at least one. Toasted, cream cheese, lox if you're feeling it.
🌆
The city sounds different at different hours Early morning UWS is quiet and lovely. Midday downtown is loud and fast. Late night in the Village is its own thing entirely. Same city, completely different places depending on when you're there.
