If You Have Time

Everything that didn't make the day pages but is worth knowing about. Pull this up when you find yourselves with an hour in a neighborhood and want to know what's nearby.

Upper West Side

Home base. Any morning with a slow start, or whenever energy allows.

Barney Greengrass
541 Amsterdam Ave at 86th
Food
The Sturgeon King — NYC's most legendary appetizing shop. Smoked salmon, sable, whitefish, eggs. Bourdain's actual Sunday morning spot when he was home. The full spread is the move.
📍 Maps
Zabar's
2245 Broadway at 80th
Food
Open since 1934. The definitive NYC appetizing and specialty grocery. Smoked fish counter, babka, coffee, kitchen gadgets upstairs. A Zabar's tote bag is a genuine New York souvenir.
📍 Maps
Levain Bakery
167 W 74th at Amsterdam
Food
The cookies that made the internet lose its mind. Enormous, dense, crispy outside and doughy inside. The chocolate chip walnut is the one. Two blocks from The Wallace.
📍 Maps
Café Lalo
201 W 83rd St at Amsterdam
FoodFilm
Beloved West Side café, over 100 desserts, the kind of place you spend two hours in without noticing. The setting for the café scenes in You've Got Mail (1998) — the table by the window.
📍 Maps

Midtown

Times Square, Grand Central, and the NYPL are on the June 9 day page as an optional end-of-day walk. One thing that didn't make it there:

Madison Square Garden
4 Penn Plaza at 33rd
The exterior. You can't go in without an event ticket but the building is unmistakable. Worth a walk-by if passing through Penn Station or Midtown on the way somewhere else.
📍 Maps

Midtown West & the High Line

June 9 post-Intrepid, if the group has energy. Pier 86 → Chelsea Market → High Line is a natural southward drift.

Chelsea Market
75 9th Ave at 15th St
Food
A former Nabisco factory (Oreos were invented here) turned indoor food hall. Long building, all vendors, easy to graze. Lobster Place (raw bar, best in the building), Doughnuttery, Ninth Street Espresso. No need to sit down — it's a walk-through.
📍 Maps
The High Line
Starts at Gansevoort St · ends at 34th St
Free
An elevated freight rail line converted into a 1.45-mile park running through the West Side. Art installations, city views, plantings that change with the season. Enter at Gansevoort (south end) after Chelsea Market and walk north. No ticket, no booking.
📍 Maps

Central Park

Beyond what's already on the June 4 and June 7 day pages. Any afternoon with time to spare.

The Boathouse
Central Park at 72nd St & East Drive
Food
Rowboat rentals on the Lake — $20/hour, cash. One of the more quintessentially New York things you can do. The café has good coffee and outdoor seating facing the water. Open daily, no reservation, just show up.
📍 Maps
Gapstow Bridge
South end of The Pond · near 59th & 5th
Film
Stone arch over The Pond, with the Plaza Hotel and 5th Avenue skyline behind it. One of the most photographed spots in the park. Home Alone 2 Pigeon Lady scenes filmed here. On the natural path from 5th Ave into the park on June 7 Track 2.
📍 Maps
Wollman Rink (Summer)
Central Park at 62nd St
In summer the ice rink converts to Victorian Gardens — a small amusement park with rides, open June through September. Low-key, families, a genuinely pleasant hour in the park. Walk-in only.
📍 Maps
Washington Mews
Between 5th Ave & University Pl · just north of WSP
A private cobblestone alley running one block behind the north side of Washington Square Park. Original 19th-century carriage houses converted to residences and NYU studios. Quiet, completely off the tourist radar, and beautiful. Walk in from 5th Ave or University Place.
📍 Maps

Greenwich Village & West Village

Extras near the June 6 Village Wander and June 8 Comedy Cellar evening.

Caffe Reggio
119 MacDougal St · next door to Comedy Cellar
Film
Open since 1927. The world's first American espresso machine lives here. Coffee, dessert, dark wood booths, framed art. Open until 3 AM. The natural before-or-after Cellar spot.

🎬 On screen The Godfather Part II (Corleone assassination scenes filmed here).
📍 Maps
Mamoun's Falafel
119 MacDougal St (multiple locations)
Food
Open since 1971, one of the oldest Middle Eastern restaurants in NYC. Falafel sandwich under $5. The original MacDougal location is a few doors from the Comedy Cellar. Cash preferred.
📍 Maps
Peanut Butter & Co.
240 Sullivan St near Washington Square
Food
Everything is peanut butter. Sandwiches, shakes, combinations that shouldn't work but do. A Village institution, beloved by NYU students for 25 years. Worth it if anyone has a sweet tooth after WSP.
📍 Maps
The Carriage House (Central Park)
59th St & Central Park South
Horse-drawn carriage rides through Central Park. Walk-up, no booking. About $100-125 for 20 minutes for the whole group. Touristy, yes — also genuinely lovely on a warm evening, especially on June 6 after Top of the Rock if energy allows.
📍 Maps
